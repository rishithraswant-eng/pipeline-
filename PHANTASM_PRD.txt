1.1 PRODUCT VISION
──────────────────
PHANTASM is a cognitive honeypot system — a next-generation active cyber
defence platform that combines generative AI network simulation, real-time
attacker behavioral profiling, and adaptive deception feedback into a single
unified system that produces actionable threat intelligence automatically.

The product vision in one sentence:
  "Turn every cyberattack into a source of intelligence, not just an
   incident to respond to."

PHANTASM operates on a philosophy that inverts the traditional security
posture. Instead of trying to keep attackers out of a decoy environment,
we make them want to stay. Instead of logging their activity and hoping an
analyst reads the log someday, we analyse their behaviour in real-time,
build a psychological profile, reshape the environment to keep them engaged
longer, and deliver a complete intelligence dossier the moment they leave.

The attacker believes they are penetrating a real Indian BFSI enterprise
network. They are not. They are inside a ghost — a generative construct that
exists only to study them. And by the time they realise this (if they ever
do), PHANTASM already knows who they are, what they wanted, what tools they
use, who they probably work for, and what their next move will be.

2.1 THE THREAT LANDSCAPE
─────────────────────────
India's cyber threat environment in 2026 is categorically different from
what it was five years ago. Three shifts define the current landscape:

SHIFT 1 — Nation-State Actors Are Now Routine Threats to Enterprise
APT-36 (Transparent Tribe, Pakistan-linked) has conducted documented
spear-phishing and credential-harvesting campaigns against Indian defence,
government, and BFSI targets continuously since at least 2016. Their
tooling sophistication has increased year-on-year. They do not stumble into
honeypots. They test environments deliberately and move on if deception is
detected. Their average reconnaissance phase against a new target lasts 90
days before initial access — they are patient, professional, and well-funded.

APT-41 (China-linked, dual espionage and criminal operations) has targeted
Indian pharmaceutical and technology companies, with particular interest in
COVID-19 research and 5G deployment data. Their operators rotate between
financially motivated attacks and state-directed espionage — the same group
operates both missions. This makes attribution from TTPs alone genuinely
complex; the same tools appear in both criminal and espionage contexts.

Lazarus Group (North Korea-linked) is primarily financially motivated against
Indian targets, with particular focus on cryptocurrency exchanges, fintech
platforms, and SWIFT-connected banking infrastructure. They have successfully
stolen hundreds of millions of dollars from financial institutions worldwide
using a combination of watering hole attacks, supply chain compromise, and
insider recruitment.

SHIFT 2 — Living-off-the-Land Makes Signature Detection Useless
Modern APT operators have largely abandoned custom malware for initial access
and lateral movement. Instead they use tools already present on the target
system: PowerShell, WMI, certutil, mshta, regsvr32. This is called
"living-off-the-land" and it is catastrophically effective against
signature-based detection because there is no malicious binary to detect.
A defender watching for known-bad executables sees nothing. The attacker
moves freely through the network using the system's own administrative tools.

SHIFT 3 — Dwell Time Remains Dangerously High
Despite billions spent on detection tools, the average attacker dwell time
in Indian enterprise networks remains around 197 days before detection
(IBM X-Force 2024). This means an attacker who compromised a network in
January is typically not discovered until July. By then, they have mapped
the entire network, exfiltrated target data, established persistence through
multiple mechanisms, and potentially compromised backup systems to defeat
recovery. The breach is catastrophic by the time anyone knows it happened.

2.2 WHY EXISTING HONEYPOTS FAIL
────────────────────────────────
The honeypot concept is sound. The implementations are broken. Here is
exactly why, from first principles:

PROBLEM 1 — Static Topology is Fingerprinted in Minutes
Every static honeypot has tells. A Honeyd installation responds to TCP SYN
packets with timing that differs from real hardware by microseconds. An
OpenCanary deployment has service banners from a fixed template library that
advanced actors have memorised. T-Pot's Cowrie SSH honeypot has been
reverse-engineered publicly and its responses documented on threat actor
forums. When an APT operator's initial probe gets a response pattern they
recognise, they do not attack. They route to real infrastructure. The honeypot
has failed its primary purpose while the attacker has learned something useful.

PROBLEM 2 — No Behavioural Intelligence Extraction
Even when an attacker does enter a static honeypot, the output is a log file.
A list of commands. Maybe a captured payload. There is no automated analysis
of what this means: who is this operator? What is their skill level? What
were they after? What other systems might they be targeting simultaneously?
A human analyst must read the log, apply expertise, and write a report.
This takes days. The intelligence is stale before it is actionable.

PROBLEM 3 — No Feedback Loop
The most fundamental failure of every existing honeypot is that it cannot
adapt. A Ransomware operator and an APT espionage operator both enter the
same static environment and see the same fake network. The ransomware operator
ignores the fake "executive workstation" because it is not relevant to their
objective. The espionage operator ignores the exposed fake database backup
because it is not what they want. Each attacker type needs a different bait
to stay engaged longest and reveal most. Static honeypots cannot provide this.
PHANTASM can. This is the core innovation.

PROBLEM 4 — Manual Reporting Breaks the Intelligence Cycle
CERT-In's 2023 directive mandates 6-hour reporting of cybersecurity incidents.
In practice, generating a compliant incident report requires a trained analyst
spending 2-4 hours documenting the incident in the required format. Most
organisations miss the 6-hour window. Many incidents go unreported entirely
because the reporting burden is too high. The intelligence that should flow
from these incidents to CERT-In's national threat picture is lost.

2.3 THE OPPORTUNITY
────────────────────
Every one of the four problems above is solvable with technology that exists
today but has not been assembled into a single operational system:

  Generative AI can produce network environments that defeat fingerprinting.
  Transformer models can classify attacker behaviour in real-time.
  Rule-based (and later RL-based) controllers can adapt the environment.
  LLMs can generate formatted compliance reports automatically.

PHANTASM assembles these capabilities into the first cognitive honeypot.

3.1 FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS — SHADOWMESH
──────────────────────────────────────────

FR-SM-01: Network Topology Generation
  The system SHALL procedurally generate a fake enterprise network topology
  on initialisation. The topology SHALL include:
  - Minimum 15 fake hosts across 3 subnets (192.168.10.x, 192.168.20.x,
    192.168.30.x) with RFC-1918 addressing
  - Hostname conventions matching Indian BFSI enterprise naming patterns
    (e.g., branch-server-01.bankname-internal.local,
    dc-auth-prod.bankname-internal.local)
  - Simulated network segmentation: user VLAN, server VLAN, management VLAN
  - At minimum one each of: domain controller, database server, web server,
    file server, payment gateway node, HR system node

FR-SM-02: Packet-Level Service Emulation
  The system SHALL intercept network packets at the kernel level using
  NFQueue and respond with protocol-correct synthetic responses for the
  following services at minimum:
  - SSH (OpenSSH banner, key exchange initiation)
  - HTTP/HTTPS (realistic web server headers, TLS handshake completion)
  - SMB/CIFS (Windows file sharing protocol responses)
  - RDP (Remote Desktop Protocol connection response)
  - LDAP/LDAPS (directory service query responses)
  - MySQL / MSSQL (database connection banner)
  - FTP (file transfer protocol banner)
  All responses SHALL match the OS and software version declared in the
  fake topology for that host to maintain internal consistency.

FR-SM-03: OS Fingerprint Consistency
  The system SHALL produce Nmap -sV -O scan results that are internally
  consistent for each fake host. A host declared as Windows Server 2019
  SHALL produce TCP/IP stack characteristics, TTL values, window sizes,
  and service response patterns consistent with Windows Server 2019.
  The system SHALL NOT produce responses that are flagged as honeypot
  by Nmap's honeypot detection NSE scripts.

FR-SM-04: Active Directory Emulation
  The system SHALL emulate a functional fake Active Directory domain
  including:
  - Kerberos authentication ticket exchange (TGT/TGS simulation)
  - LDAP directory queries returning fake but structurally correct responses
  - Procedurally generated user accounts (minimum 100) with realistic
    Indian names, employee IDs, department assignments
  - Group Policy Object enumeration responses
  - Domain trust and forest structure responses
  The AD emulation is critical because lateral movement via credential abuse
  is the most common APT technique. An attacker who cannot interact with
  what appears to be real AD will immediately suspect a honeypot.

FR-SM-05: Background Traffic Generation
  The system SHALL generate continuous synthetic background network traffic
  to simulate a live enterprise environment. Required traffic types:
  - NTP synchronisation packets from all fake hosts at realistic intervals
  - DNS query/response traffic between fake hosts and fake DNS server
  - SMB keep-alive packets between fake workstations
  - LDAP authentication attempts at realistic frequency
  - HTTPS traffic from fake workstations to fake internet proxy
  Background traffic volume SHALL match statistical profiles from the
  CICIDS-2023 dataset for a 100-employee enterprise network.
  Rationale: A passive attacker performing traffic analysis on an enterprise
  network they have partially compromised will notice the absence of
  background traffic. This silence is a primary honeypot tell that
  ShadowMesh must defeat.

FR-SM-06: Dynamic Topology Mutation
  The system SHALL accept topology mutation directives from the feedback
  controller and materialise new network nodes within 5 seconds of
  receiving the directive. Mutation SHALL be additive only (new nodes
  appear — existing nodes do not disappear, as disappearing infrastructure
  would alert the attacker). New nodes SHALL be fully emulated with all
  services appropriate to their declared role.

FR-SM-07: Response Latency Randomisation
  The system SHALL randomise response latencies for all fake hosts within
  a gaussian distribution centred on a host-specific baseline value.
  Baseline values SHALL be assigned per host based on declared hardware
  type (servers: 1-3ms, workstations: 2-8ms, network appliances: <1ms).
  Jitter SHALL be applied as a ±30% variation around baseline.
  Rationale: Consistent sub-millisecond response from all hosts on a
  subnet is a primary timing-analysis honeypot fingerprint. Real hardware
  has variable, hardware-dependent response characteristics.

3.2 FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS — MIRRORTRAP
──────────────────────────────────────────

FR-MT-01: Command Ingestion Pipeline
  The system SHALL capture all commands issued by the attacker in any
  interactive session (SSH, RDP command execution, PowerShell remoting,
  web shell execution) and deliver them to the profiling engine with
  millisecond-precision timestamps within 100ms of execution.

FR-MT-02: MITRE ATT&CK Technique Classification
  The system SHALL classify each attacker command or command sequence
  into one or more MITRE ATT&CK v14 technique IDs using a fine-tuned
  DistilBERT classification model. Requirements:
  - Classification SHALL complete within 500ms per command
  - The model SHALL handle multi-technique commands (one command may
    trigger multiple ATT&CK technique tags simultaneously)
  - Classification confidence scores SHALL be exposed for all predictions
  - Minimum 75% accuracy on the test command sequence defined in
    Appendix A of this document
  - The model SHALL be pre-trained and loaded at startup, requiring
    no runtime training

FR-MT-03: Expertise Classification
  The system SHALL maintain a continuously updated expertise classification
  for the active session, expressed as one of four levels:
  [Script Kiddie | Intermediate | Advanced | APT-Grade]

  Classification SHALL be derived from the following observable signals:
  - Command complexity score: weighted average of command length, pipe
    count, subshell usage, redirection operators per command
  - Error recovery pattern: time from error to successful retry, method
    of retry (exact repeat vs. modified approach vs. alternative method)
  - Tool sophistication: whether operator uses native OS tools, known
    frameworks, or novel custom tooling
  - Sequencing logic: whether commands follow a logical penetration testing
    methodology or appear random/template-driven
  - Privilege escalation speed: time from initial access to first
    privilege escalation attempt (faster = more experienced)

  The expertise classification SHALL update after every 5 commands and
  SHALL only move upward during a session (an Advanced attacker who
  makes an error does not get downgraded to Intermediate).

FR-MT-04: Objective Inference
  The system SHALL infer the attacker's primary objective from their
  behaviour pattern, expressed as one of:
  [Ransomware | Data Exfiltration | Espionage | Sabotage |
   Cryptomining | Insider Threat | Financial Fraud | Unknown]

  Inference SHALL use the following behavioural signals:
  - Scanning behaviour: breadth-first (wide, shallow = ransomware,
    cryptomining) vs. depth-first (narrow, deep = espionage, exfil)
  - Data access pattern: bulk read operations vs. targeted specific
    record lookups
  - Persistence mechanism: which persistence techniques attempted
    reveals operational intent (ransomware needs no persistence,
    espionage requires durable implants)
  - Lateral movement targeting: which systems the attacker prioritises
    reveals what they value (HR = insider, SWIFT = financial fraud)
  - Encryption preparation: shadow copy deletion, backup enumeration
    = ransomware with high confidence

FR-MT-05: Operational State Estimation
  The system SHALL estimate the attacker operator's real-time cognitive
  and emotional state using inter-command timing analysis:

  Timing metrics computed in real-time:
  - Mean inter-command interval (ICI) for rolling 20-command window
  - ICI standard deviation (consistency indicator)
  - ICI trend slope (accelerating = stress, decelerating = confusion)
  - Long-pause events: ICI > 45 seconds flagged as "external consultation"
  - Burst events: ICI < 1 second for 3+ consecutive commands = automation

  State classifications:
  - Calm/Methodical: ICI 5-30s, low variance, stable trend
  - Stressed/Rushed: ICI decreasing trend, high acceleration coefficient
  - Disoriented: high ICI variance, filler command clusters (whoami, pwd)
  - Automated: ICI < 2s consistently, near-zero variance

FR-MT-06: Attribution Signal Extraction
  The system SHALL compute a TTP overlap score between the observed
  session's technique vector and the published technique vectors for
  all 138 MITRE ATT&CK named threat groups using cosine similarity.

  Additionally, the system SHALL extract the following attribution signals:
  - Tool signature matching: compare tool usage patterns against known
    APT toolkit fingerprints (Cobalt Strike, Mimikatz, custom Python
    implants, known RAT families)
  - Command syntax idiom analysis: PowerShell quoting styles, bash
    variable expansion patterns, encoding choices
  - Session timing correlation: active hours mapped to timezone grid
    to infer operator geographic region

  Attribution output SHALL be a ranked probability distribution with
  confidence scores. Groups with >60% overlap SHALL trigger an
  immediate high-priority alert flag in the dashboard.

FR-MT-07: Profile Update Frequency and Latency
  The system SHALL publish profile updates to the Redis channel within
  200ms of the 5-command trigger threshold being crossed. The dashboard
  SHALL receive and render the update within 100ms of publication.
  Total latency from command execution to profile card update on
  dashboard SHALL not exceed 500ms end-to-end.

3.3 FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS — FEEDBACK CONTROLLER
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

FR-FC-01: Profile Subscription and Trigger Logic
  The feedback controller SHALL subscribe to the MirrorTrap Redis
  channel and evaluate every profile update against a set of trigger
  conditions. Trigger conditions and corresponding actions:

  TRIGGER 1 — APT/Advanced Operator Detected
  Condition: Expertise = Advanced or APT-Grade AND Objective = Espionage
             or Data Exfiltration AND confidence > 80%
  Action: Issue directive to ShadowMesh to materialise crown jewel nodes:
          FINACLE-PROD-DB (port 1521, Oracle emulation)
          SWIFT-INTERFACE-GW (port 8443, HTTPS with MQ banner)
          EXECUTIVE-WS-CFO (port 3389, RDP with 2FA prompt simulation)
          UID-API-INTERNAL (port 443, REST endpoint returning fake Aadhaar
          validation responses)

  TRIGGER 2 — Ransomware Operator Detected
  Condition: Objective = Ransomware AND any shadow copy enumeration
             command detected
  Action: Materialise backup infrastructure nodes:
          BACKUP-SRV-01 (exposed SMB with fake *.bak files)
          VEEAM-PROXY (port 9392, Veeam backup service banner)
          NAS-STORAGE-01 (exposed NFS shares with fake DB dump files)

  TRIGGER 3 — Insider Threat Pattern Detected
  Condition: Objective = Insider Threat AND credential-only lateral movement
             (no scanning, no exploitation attempts detected)
  Action: Materialise sensitive HR and executive data nodes:
          HR-DB-PROD (MSSQL with fake employee salary schema)
          LEGAL-FILESHARE (SMB with fake legal document structure)
          EXEC-SHAREPOINT (HTTPS with fake board minutes and compensation data)

  TRIGGER 4 — Stress Event Detected
  Condition: Operational state transitions to Stressed/Rushed from any state
  Action: Slightly slow ShadowMesh response latencies by 20-40ms to
          increase attacker uncertainty and prolong session dwell time.
          Add a fake "partially restricted" access prompt to the next
          service the attacker probes to create the illusion of hitting
          a real security control.

FR-FC-02: Engagement Score Tracking
  The feedback controller SHALL maintain an engagement score for each
  session, defined as:
    Engagement Score = (actual dwell time) / (projected dwell time in
    a static honeypot for this attacker profile)

  Projected dwell time for a static honeypot is estimated at:
    Script Kiddie: 8 minutes (automated tools, quick exit)
    Intermediate: 15 minutes (some manual interaction, then exit)
    Advanced: 4 minutes (quick fingerprint, avoid)
    APT-Grade: 2 minutes (near-instant fingerprint, avoid)

  An engagement score >1.0 means PHANTASM is outperforming a static
  honeypot. This metric SHALL be displayed prominently on the dashboard.

FR-FC-03: Mutation Rate Limiting
  The system SHALL not issue more than one topology mutation directive
  per 120 seconds to avoid making topology changes fast enough that an
  attacker notices the environment is changing. Real enterprise networks
  change slowly. An infrastructure node appearing within 10 seconds
  of the attacker probing a new subnet would be suspicious. The 120-second
  window gives enough time for the attacker to organically "discover"
  new nodes through their own scanning.

3.4 FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS — DOSSIER ENGINE
───────────────────────────────────────────────

FR-DE-01: Session Aggregation
  On session end, the dossier engine SHALL compile a complete structured
  session record containing:
  - Full chronological command log with millisecond timestamps
  - Profile state snapshots at every 5-command checkpoint
  - Complete ATT&CK technique list with first-observed timestamps
  - Tool identifications with version where determinable
  - All fake assets accessed and interaction depth per asset
  - All C2 callback attempts with destination IP, port, and payload hash
  - Engagement score and dwell time comparison
  - Attribution probability distribution at session end

FR-DE-02: Narrative Generation
  The system SHALL pass the structured session record to the LLM API
  with a carefully engineered system prompt that produces a narrative
  analysis section in the dossier. The narrative SHALL:
  - Describe the attack methodology in plain English readable by a
    non-technical CERT-In analyst
  - Identify the 3 most significant behavioural observations
  - Note any anomalies from typical attacker patterns for the identified
    profile type
  - Comment on the stress event at minute 31 (or equivalent actual
    observed event) with interpretation
  - Provide attribution reasoning with explicit confidence caveats

FR-DE-03: CERT-In MIS Format Compliance
  The dossier SHALL be structured to contain all fields required by the
  CERT-In Mandatory Information Security (MIS) incident report format:
  - Incident type classification
  - Affected systems (fake, clearly marked as deception environment)
  - Attack vector
  - Indicators of Compromise (IOCs): tool hashes, C2 IPs, domain names
  - Timeline of events
  - Recommended actions
  - Organisation details (pre-filled template)

FR-DE-04: Generation Time
  The complete dossier, including LLM narrative generation and PDF
  rendering, SHALL complete within 60 seconds of session end trigger.
  This is a hard requirement for demo purposes.

FR-DE-05: Physical Output
  The system SHALL produce a PDF-rendered dossier that can be printed
  and physically handed to a judge during the demo. The PDF SHALL be
  formatted professionally with PHANTASM branding, classified markings,
  and structured sections. This physical artefact is a critical demo
  component — it makes the output tangible.

3.5 FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS — DASHBOARD
─────────────────────────────────────────

FR-DB-01: Real-Time Command Feed
  The dashboard SHALL display a live scrolling feed of attacker commands
  as they are executed. Each command entry SHALL show:
  - Timestamp (HH:MM:SS.mmm)
  - The command text (truncated to 120 characters if longer)
  - ATT&CK technique tag(s) as a coloured badge
  - Confidence score of the classification
  Colour coding by severity category:
    Discovery/Reconnaissance: blue (#3B82F6)
    Credential Access: amber (#F59E0B)
    Lateral Movement: orange (#EF4444 at 70% opacity)
    Exfiltration/C2: red (#EF4444)
    Execution: yellow (#EAB308)
  New commands SHALL appear at the bottom with a subtle slide-in animation.

FR-DB-02: Network Topology Graph
  The dashboard SHALL render the ShadowMesh topology as an interactive
  D3.js force-directed graph with the following behaviours:
  - Nodes appear with a materialisation animation (scale from 0, glow pulse)
  - Nodes pulse amber when actively being probed
  - Nodes turn red when successfully accessed by attacker
  - Edges animate directionally to show lateral movement paths
  - New nodes added by the feedback loop appear with a distinctive
    "emergence" animation (node grows from a pulse point)
  - Clicking a node shows its fake service details in a side panel
  - The graph SHALL re-layout smoothly (animated force simulation) when
    new nodes are added without disorienting the existing view

FR-DB-03: Operator Profile Card
  The dashboard SHALL display a live profile card with four gauge
  components updating in real-time:
  - Expertise gauge: segmented arc from Script Kiddie to APT-Grade
  - Objective confidence bars: horizontal bars for each objective type
  - Operational state indicator: icon + colour coded status chip
  - Attribution radar chart: spider/radar showing top 5 APT group overlaps
  All four components SHALL animate smoothly on each profile update.
  The profile card SHALL also show current session statistics:
  commands issued, techniques observed, dwell time elapsed, engagement score.

FR-DB-04: Stress Index Timeline
  The dashboard SHALL render a live line chart showing the attacker's
  inter-command timing (ICI) plotted over session time. The chart SHALL:

  - Mark significant events (stress onset, disorientation, long pauses)
    with vertical annotation markers
  - Use amber colouring for the stress threshold zone (ICI < 3 seconds
    for consecutive commands)

  - Dossier generation trigger button (also fires automatically on

  - Engagement score real-time display with baseline comparison

  - Command ingestion to ATT&CK classification: <500ms (hard limit)
  - Profile update publish to Redis: <200ms

  - Network graph re-render after data update: <16ms (60fps target)

NFR-02: RELIABILITY
────────────────────
  - The demo pipeline MUST survive a 10-minute uninterrupted session
    without crash, hang, or unhandled exception
  - All components SHALL have exception handling that logs errors to
    a debug file rather than crashing the process
  - Redis connection loss SHALL trigger automatic reconnection with
    exponential backoff (1s, 2s, 4s) rather than system halt
  - WebSocket disconnection SHALL trigger automatic client reconnection
    with graceful state restoration
  - The demo SHALL have a documented recovery procedure for each component
    failure scenario that restores full demo capability in under 60 seconds

NFR-03: SECURITY OF PHANTASM ITSELF
──────────────────────────────────────
  Note: PHANTASM is a security tool and must not itself be trivially
  compromised. For hackathon purposes:
  - All PHANTASM management interfaces (dashboard, session controls)
    SHALL be bound to localhost only
  - The ShadowMesh NFQueue hook SHALL only process packets on a designated
    network interface, not the management interface
  - No real credentials of any kind SHALL appear anywhere in PHANTASM's
    codebase, configuration files, or generated outputs
  - All fake credentials generated for the AD emulation SHALL be clearly
    marked as synthetic in internal data structures

NFR-04: PORTABILITY
────────────────────
  - The complete PHANTASM system SHALL run on a single laptop running
    Ubuntu 22.04 LTS or higher
  - All dependencies SHALL be installable via pip and npm with no
    system package requirements beyond what ships with Ubuntu
  - Exception: libnetfilter_queue requires apt install. This SHALL be
    documented in the setup script and executed in pre-demo setup.
  - Docker SHALL be used for ShadowMesh service containers to ensure
    consistent behaviour regardless of host configuration
  - The system SHALL not require internet connectivity during demo
    execution. All model weights, datasets, and dependencies SHALL
    be pre-loaded in the hackathon environment.

NFR-05: DEMO STABILITY
───────────────────────
  This is the most important non-functional requirement for a hackathon.
  A system that is 80% functional but crashes during the demo scores zero.
  A system that is 60% functional but runs flawlessly for 90 seconds wins.

  - Every component SHALL have a documented "demo mode" configuration that
    reduces scope to the minimum needed for the demo loop to work
  - The command sequence used in the demo SHALL be rehearsed minimum 5 times
    on the exact hardware used for the demo before the presentation
  - A backup command sequence SHALL be pre-scripted in a separate terminal
    window, ready to run if live interaction fails
  - All LLM API calls SHALL be wrapped with a cached fallback — if the API
    call fails, the system returns a pre-generated dossier for the demo
    profile. The judge will not see the difference.

5.1 SYSTEM COMPONENTS AND INTERACTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────

COMPONENT 1 — ShadowMesh Core (sm_core.py)
  Responsibilities:
    - Topology generation and state management
    - NFQueue packet interception and dispatch
    - Per-service response handler registry
    - Background traffic emission
    - Mutation directive application
  Interfaces:
    - Exposes REST API on localhost:8001 for topology queries
    - Subscribes to Redis channel "sm:mutations" for directives
    - Publishes probe events to Redis channel "sm:events"

COMPONENT 2 — Service Emulation Library (sm_services/)
  One module per service family:
    ssh_emulator.py — OpenSSH banner, key exchange
    smb_emulator.py — SMB2 protocol responses
    rdp_emulator.py — RDP connection sequence
    ldap_emulator.py — LDAP/LDAPS directory responses
    http_emulator.py — HTTP/HTTPS responses with fake application content
    db_emulator.py — MySQL/MSSQL/Oracle connection banners
    ad_emulator.py — Kerberos TGT/TGS, LDAP AD-specific responses
  Each emulator reads the host configuration from the topology state and
  returns a response appropriate to that host's declared specifications.

COMPONENT 3 — GAN Traffic Generator (sm_traffic_gan.py)
  Responsibilities:
    - Load pre-trained DCGAN model weights at startup
    - Generate synthetic inter-host traffic packets at configured volume
    - Emit packets on the designated network interface via raw sockets
  The GAN model is trained offline on CICIDS-2023 traffic and weights
  are bundled with the repository (no training at runtime).

COMPONENT 4 — MirrorTrap Engine (mt_engine.py)
  Responsibilities:
    - Receive commands from ShadowMesh via Redis "sm:events" channel
    - Tokenise and batch commands for model inference
    - Run BERT-base classifier for ATT&CK technique prediction
    - Run DistilBERT expert-level classifier for expertise scoring
    - Compute inter-command timing statistics
    - Run cosine similarity attribution scoring against ATT&CK group vectors
    - Publish profile updates to Redis "mt:profile" channel

COMPONENT 5 — Feedback Controller (fc_controller.py)
  Responsibilities:
    - Subscribe to Redis "mt:profile" channel
    - Evaluate trigger conditions on every profile update
    - Issue topology mutation directives to Redis "sm:mutations"
    - Maintain engagement score calculation
    - Enforce mutation rate limiting (120-second minimum interval)

COMPONENT 6 — Session Manager (session_mgr.py)
  Responsibilities:
    - Manage session lifecycle: start, active, ended
    - Aggregate session data from all channels
    - Trigger dossier generation on session end
    - Provide session history API for dashboard

COMPONENT 7 — Dossier Engine (de_engine.py)
  Responsibilities:
    - Receive session record from session manager
    - Format structured data for LLM prompt
    - Call LLM API with retry logic and cached fallback
    - Parse LLM response and embed in dossier template
    - Render PDF via WeasyPrint
    - Expose generated PDF via localhost file server for download

COMPONENT 8 — API Server (api_server.py)
  Responsibilities:
    - FastAPI application serving REST endpoints for dashboard
    - WebSocket endpoint for real-time push to frontend
    - Bridge between Redis pub/sub and WebSocket clients
    - Session control endpoints (start, stop, manual triggers)

COMPONENT 9 — Frontend Dashboard (React SPA)
  Responsibilities:
    - WebSocket connection to API server
    - D3.js network graph rendering
    - Profile card and gauge components
    - Command feed with ATT&CK classification badges
    - Session statistics and engagement score display
    - Dossier download and print trigger

5.2 DATA FLOW
─────────────

ATTACK SEQUENCE DATA FLOW:

  1. Attacker sends packet to ShadowMesh IP
  2. NFQueue intercepts packet at kernel level
  3. sm_core.py dispatches to appropriate service emulator
  4. Service emulator crafts protocol-correct response
  5. Response returned via NFQueue, delivered to attacker
  6. Command/probe event published to Redis "sm:events"
  7. mt_engine.py consumes event from Redis
  8. Command tokenised, inference run, technique classified
  9. Timing statistics updated, profile state updated
  10. Profile update published to Redis "mt:profile"
  11. fc_controller.py evaluates triggers, issues mutation if threshold met
  12. api_server.py bridges profile update to WebSocket
  13. Frontend receives update, renders animated UI change
  14. If mutation issued: sm_core.py applies new topology nodes
  15. New nodes reflected in dashboard graph via same event path

DOSSIER GENERATION DATA FLOW:

  1. Session end detected (attacker disconnect or manual trigger)
  2. session_mgr.py compiles complete session record
  3. de_engine.py receives record, formats LLM prompt
  4. LLM API call (with 30-second timeout and cached fallback)
  5. LLM returns narrative text
  6. de_engine.py assembles full dossier (structured + narrative)
  7. WeasyPrint renders HTML template to PDF
  8. PDF saved to output directory
  9. Dashboard receives "dossier_ready" event via WebSocket
  10. Download link activates on dashboard

6.2 RISK REGISTER
──────────────────

RISK 1 — NFQueue permissions issue on demo hardware
  Probability: Medium | Impact: Critical
  Mitigation: Test NFQueue permissions on demo laptop during Phase 0.
  Fallback: Use libpcap + raw sockets for packet response instead of
  NFQueue. Less elegant but functionally equivalent for demo purposes.

RISK 2 — BERT inference too slow on CPU
  Probability: Medium | Impact: High
  Mitigation: Load ONNX-quantised model (INT8 quantisation). Test
  inference time in Phase 0. Target <500ms on 4-core laptop CPU.
  Fallback: If still slow, batch classification runs every 10 commands
  instead of 5. Less responsive but still demonstrable.

RISK 3 — LLM API failure during demo
  Probability: Low | Impact: High
  Mitigation: Pre-generate 3 high-quality dossiers for the demo profile.
  Cache them with hash keys matching expected session signatures. If API
  call fails, cached dossier returns in under 1 second.

RISK 4 — D3 graph re-layout too slow with many nodes
  Probability: Low | Impact: Medium
  Mitigation: Limit graph to 25 nodes maximum. Pre-compute layout for
  base topology. Only force-simulate new nodes, not entire graph.

RISK 5 — WebSocket instability under demo conditions
  Probability: Low | Impact: High
  Mitigation: Implement automatic reconnection. Test 100 rapid consecutive
  updates before demo. If WebSocket fails, API server serves polling
  endpoint as fallback for dashboard data refresh.

7.2 PROMPT ENGINEERING SPECIFICATION
──────────────────────────────────────

The LLM prompt SHALL use the following structure for consistent,
high-quality narrative generation:

SYSTEM PROMPT:
  "You are a senior threat intelligence analyst at CERT-In with 15 years
   of experience analysing APT operations against Indian critical
   infrastructure. You write concise, precise, jargon-appropriate threat
   intelligence reports that can be read by both technical security
   professionals and senior government officials. You do not speculate
   beyond what the evidence supports. You cite specific observable
   behaviours as the basis for every analytical conclusion. You never
   use vague language like 'possibly' or 'might' — either the evidence
   supports a conclusion or you state the confidence level explicitly.
   Write in formal Indian English."

USER PROMPT STRUCTURE:
  "Analyse the following session data from a PHANTASM cognitive honeypot
   engagement and produce a 400-word threat intelligence narrative for
   inclusion in a CERT-In MIS incident report.
   
   Session data: {structured_json}
   
   Your narrative must:
   1. Open with the single most important finding from this session
   2. Describe the attack methodology chronologically using the technique
      timeline provided
   3. Explain the stress event at {stress_onset_time} with your
      interpretation of what caused it
   4. State attribution conclusions with explicit confidence levels
   5. End with one specific, actionable recommendation for Indian BFSI
      defenders based on this operator's demonstrated TTPs
   
   Do not include headers. Write as continuous analytical prose.
   Do not repeat data already shown in the structured fields.
   Output only the narrative text, nothing else."

9.1 VERSION 2.0 — RL TOPOLOGY OPTIMISATION
────────────────────────────────────────────
The hackathon implements rule-based topology mutation. Version 2.0 replaces
the rules with a trained Reinforcement Learning agent.

Environment: The PHANTASM ghost network is the environment.
Agent: The feedback controller becomes an RL policy.
Action space: Discrete set of topology mutations (add node type X, slow
response latency by Y%, expose vulnerability class Z).
State: The current MirrorTrap profile vector + session statistics.
Reward: Attacker dwell time extension per action. Negative reward for
actions that cause the attacker to disconnect (overexposure of obvious
fake infrastructure).

Training: Simulated attacker agents (scripted bots representing each
attacker archetype) train the RL policy in simulation. The trained policy
is then deployed against real attackers.

Algorithm: MAPPO (Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimisation) if we model
the system as multi-agent (one agent per attacker type). PPO single-agent
if we train a generalised policy.

9.2 VERSION 2.0 — MULTI-SESSION OPERATOR TRACKING
────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The same human operator often probes the same target multiple times over
days or weeks with different tools, from different IP addresses. Version 2.0
implements cross-session correlation to recognise returning operators.

Operator fingerprint: A vector combining tool signature patterns, command
syntax idioms, timing profile characteristics, and ATT&CK technique
preference patterns. This fingerprint is stable across sessions even when
source IP, tools, and entry method change.

Storage: Operator fingerprint vectors stored in a vector database
(ChromaDB or Qdrant). New sessions compared against historical fingerprints
using approximate nearest-neighbour search. Match threshold: cosine
similarity >0.82.

9.3 VERSION 3.0 — FEDERATED NATIONAL DEPLOYMENT
──────────────────────────────────────────────────
Multiple PHANTASM instances deployed at different organisations share
anonymised attacker profiles — tool signatures, TTP vectors, timing
patterns — without sharing any organisation-specific information.

When one PHANTASM instance profiles an operator with high APT-36 overlap,
every other instance in the federation immediately receives an alert and
pre-configures its adaptive responses for that operator type.

This creates a national-level early warning system from distributed
deception infrastructure — the aggregated intelligence picture is
qualitatively richer than any single instance could produce.

Architecture: Central federation server receives anonymised profile vectors
via encrypted channel. No command logs, no IP addresses, no organisation
identifiers shared. Only the statistical signature of the operator type.
CERT-In operates the federation server.

10.1 LEGALITY OF HONEYPOT OPERATIONS
──────────────────────────────────────
Honeypot operation is legal in India under the Information Technology Act
2000 and its 2008 amendments. An organisation may deploy deceptive
infrastructure on its own network to detect and study intrusion attempts.
There is no legal obligation to inform attackers that they are in a
deception environment.

PHANTASM does not:
  - Access any real systems belonging to the attacker
  - Execute any offensive code on attacker infrastructure
  - Attempt to attribute or identify real individuals (attribution is
    probabilistic and advisory, not definitive or used for prosecution)
  - Store or process any real user personal data

PHANTASM does:
  - Operate entirely on infrastructure controlled by the deploying organisation
  - Generate synthetic data that never corresponds to real individuals
  - Produce intelligence reports for internal defensive use and regulatory
    submission, not for offensive action

10.2 RESPONSIBLE DISCLOSURE
────────────────────────────
IOCs (C2 IPs, tool hashes) captured by PHANTASM should be shared with
CERT-In and relevant sector CERTs via the MISP threat sharing platform
following responsible disclosure principles. PHANTASM's dossier format
is designed to make this submission straightforward.

10.3 DATA HANDLING
────────────────────
All fake data generated by PHANTASM (employee names, account details, file
contents) is entirely synthetic and generated by the Faker library with no
connection to real individuals. The synthetic nature of all data SHALL be
clearly marked in all internal data structures and documentation.
No real credentials or personal data of any kind SHALL appear in
PHANTASM's codebase, configuration, or outputs.
